1. Purpose

The objective of this SOP is to create a safe operating environment for all researchers in the EA Program laboratories. It establishes general safety rules, emergency procedures, training requirements, and record-keeping procedures.

This Laboratory Safety Plan is a living document and requires ongoing updating, editing, and expansion as incidents occur and lessons are learned.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All staff, students, and researchers working in EA Program laboratories and field sites
  • Does not apply to: Visitors on supervised tours who do not handle materials or equipment
  • Location: SGM 133 and all associated EA Program spaces

4. Definitions

Laboratory Manager: A student, staff, or faculty member who has been trained and identified as the laboratory manager. The laboratory manager oversees all training and monitors all activities in the laboratory. The current laboratory manager is Assoc. Professor Marc Los Huertos.

Chemical Safety Officer: Assigned annually; responsible for maintaining SOPs, training researchers, and ensuring safe laboratory conditions (see SOP-L02).

PPE (Personal Protective Equipment): Protective clothing, goggles, gloves, or other garments designed to protect the wearer from injury or infection.

SDS (Safety Data Sheet): A document describing the hazards, safe handling, storage, disposal, and first aid for each chemical used in the lab. The SDS binder is located on the counter next to the east entrance to SGM 133.

6. Health and Safety

6.1 General Safety Rules

  • Developing a safe and productive environment is key to sustaining a strong scientific ethic; reflect on how you and your colleagues can promote safe conditions
  • All personnel must always employ a buddy system, whether working within or outside traditional work hours (8 AM – 5 PM), and on weekends
  • You must obtain written permission to work in the lab outside traditional hours
  • No work is allowed in the laboratory without expressed permission from the Laboratory Manager
  • No food, drink, or cosmetics in the laboratory
  • No headphones or earbuds that block auditory hazard warnings
  • Long hair must be tied back; loose clothing and dangling jewelry must be secured
  • Wash hands thoroughly before leaving the laboratory
  • Report all accidents, injuries, and near-misses to the lab manager immediately

7.1 Earthquakes

Earthquakes are common in southern California. Developing an effective strategy before an earthquake strikes is key to laboratory safety.

Indoor response:

  1. Move away from windows, filing cabinets, shelves, chemicals, and other heavy objects that may fall
  2. “Duck, Cover, and Hold” until shaking stops
  3. If not near a table or sturdy desk, crouch against an interior wall and cover your neck and head
  4. Once shaking stops, take any necessary items (phone, wallet, medication) and leave the building
  5. Go to the nearest Pomona College evacuation site and wait for further instructions
  6. Put on shoes as there could be broken debris
  7. DO NOT USE ELEVATORS

Outdoor response:

  1. Move away from buildings, trees, signs, electrical poles, and wires
  2. Protect your head and neck
  3. After shaking stops, go to the nearest evacuation site

Aftershocks may occur. Be prepared and follow appropriate evacuation plans if they do.

7.2 Fires and Evacuation

In the case of a small fire, you may attempt to use a fire extinguisher. Remember:

  1. Remove people from the area
  2. Activate the closest fire alarm
  3. Call Campus Safety
  4. Extinguish the fire only if safe to do so:
    • Place yourself between the fire and the exit
    • Pull the pin
    • Aim the nozzle toward the base of the fire
    • Squeeze the handle to release the extinguishing agent
    • Sweep the base of the fire from side to side
  5. Use only one fire extinguisher; if the fire is not controlled after one, leave the room, close the door, and evacuate

If trapped in a building:

  1. Call Campus Safety
  2. Close all windows and doors
  3. Wet and place cloth material around and under the door to prevent smoke from entering

7.3 Medical Emergencies

  1. Call Campus Safety; let them know the victim’s condition
  2. Provide necessary first aid until Campus Safety and/or emergency services arrive (only if trained)
  3. Do not move the injured person unless necessary
  4. See the campus evacuation map for AED locations

7.4 Chemical Spills

If there is a hazardous chemical spill in the laboratory, do not attempt to clean it up unless trained and equipped to do so. Refer to SOP-L02 Hazardous Materials Handling for spill response procedures.

  • If chemicals get in your eye, flush for 15 minutes at the eyewash station
  • If toxic materials are spilled on your body, remove all clothing and stand beneath the lab shower for 15 minutes
  • In the event of inhalation, move to fresh air
  • If someone is at risk, protect yourself first; only provide assistance if you can do so safely

7.5 Crime on Campus

Active shooter: Exit the building if safe to do so. If not, close and lock the laboratory door, turn off lights, pull down window shades, keep quiet, silence phones, and call Campus Safety.

Bomb threat and utilities failure: Review the Emergency Brochure posted near laboratory exits for detailed procedures.

8. Training Requirements

8.1 Required Training for All Researchers

A researcher is not permitted to use lab equipment until he or she has been appropriately trained. Training is complete only after the Training Documentation Form (Appendix A) has been signed by both the researcher and the lab manager.

Every researcher must complete the following before beginning independent work:

Order Training Item SOP Reference
1 General Laboratory Safety (this SOP) L01
2 Hazardous Materials Handling L02
3 Foreign Soil Handling (all users, even if not using foreign soils) F41 (needs conversion)
4 Field Safety (if conducting field work) F01
5 Pomona College Lab Safety Course Annual requirement
6 Pomona College Handbook on Sexual Harassment Required reading
7 Project-specific SOPs As assigned by PI

8.2 Training Procedure

  1. Read the assigned SOP thoroughly
  2. Discuss the SOP with the lab manager, including hazards, mitigation strategies, and emergency procedures
  3. Demonstrate competency for any hands-on procedures (if applicable)
  4. Sign and date the SOP Training Documentation Form (Appendix A)
  5. Obtain the lab manager’s signature verifying your training
  6. Place the completed form in the SOP Training Binder in the lab

8.3 Annual Requirements

  • All researchers must attend the Pomona College Lab Safety Course each year they are working in the lab
  • Training sign-offs must be renewed when SOPs are revised to a new version
  • The lab manager documents the completion date of the annual safety course

8.4 Estimated Time

Initial safety training (this SOP plus discussion) generally requires approximately 45 minutes.

9. Housekeeping

  • When you arrive, look around to identify any safety concerns and report them immediately to the Lab Manager
  • Keep work areas clean and organized
  • Return all chemicals and equipment to their designated storage locations after use
  • Clean up spills immediately using appropriate procedures
  • Dispose of waste in the correct containers (chemical waste, sharps, biohazard, general trash)
  • Do not block emergency exits, safety showers, or eyewash stations
  • Report broken or malfunctioning equipment to the lab manager

10. Ethical Efforts and Contributions

Working in the lab requires thinking carefully about what you are contributing and how others using the lab will find it. Each researcher is expected to leave the lab in better condition than they found it and to contribute to the ongoing improvement of safety documentation.
