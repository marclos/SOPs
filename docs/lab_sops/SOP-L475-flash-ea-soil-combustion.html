Flash EA Soil Combustion for IRMS Analysis

SOP-L475 Revision 1.1
Author

Marc Los Huertos

Environmental Analysis, Pomona College

Kyle McCarty

Oxtoby Isotope Lab, Pomona College

Published

2026-06-07

1 Purpose

This SOP describes the complete workflow for analyzing soil and sediment samples for \(\delta^{15}\)N, \(\delta^{13}\)C, and elemental C and N content using the Thermo Scientific Flash IRMS Elemental Analyzer (EA) coupled to the Delta V Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometer (IRMS) through the ConFlo IV continuous-flow interface (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2008b, 2010a). It covers sample preparation, instrument warm-up, sequence creation, running the analysis, shutdown, and data reduction.

For general instrument operation, gas checks, and pre-analysis tests, see SOP-L208. For the full data correction workflow (blank, linearity, scale normalization, and drift correction), see SOP-L414.

2 Scope

  • Applies to: Soil, sediment, and plant tissue samples analyzed for \(\delta^{13}\)C, \(\delta^{15}\)N, and elemental concentrations (%C, %N) by combustion EA-IRMS in NC (dual reactor) mode
  • Does not apply to: Sulfur isotope analysis (NCS mode), GasBench carbonate analysis (see SOP-L415), TC/EA hydrogen and oxygen isotope analysis, or liquid samples
  • Location: Sample preparation in SGM Rm 133; analysis in the Oxtoby Environmental Isotope Lab, SGM Rm 135

4 Definitions

  • Blank correction: Subtraction of the isotopic and elemental contribution of an empty tin capsule from each sample signal; necessary because capsule tin contributes a small amount of C and N (Paul et al., 2007; Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010a).
  • Certified user: A student, staff, or faculty member qualified to prepare samples and run the IRMS without supervision, as designated by the lab PI.
  • ConFlo IV: Interface hub connecting the Flash EA and other peripherals to the Delta V IRMS; handles reference-gas delivery and peak switching (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2008a).
  • \(\delta^{13}\)C: Stable carbon isotope ratio expressed in per mil (‰) relative to Vienna Pee Dee Belemnite (VPDB): \(\delta^{13}C = (R_\text{sample}/R_\text{standard} - 1) \times 1000\) where \(R = {}^{13}C/{}^{12}C\) (Coplen, 2011).
  • \(\delta^{15}\)N: Stable nitrogen isotope ratio expressed in per mil (‰) relative to atmospheric N2 (AIR): \(\delta^{15}N = (R_\text{sample}/R_\text{standard} - 1) \times 1000\) where \(R = {}^{15}N/{}^{14}N\) (Coplen, 2011).
  • Drift correction: Adjustment for systematic temporal change in measured \(\delta\) values during a sequence, estimated from bracketing reference materials (Paul et al., 2007).
  • Flash EA: The Thermo Scientific Flash IRMS Elemental Analyzer peripheral; combusts samples at 1020 °C in an O2 pulse and separates N2 and CO2 chromatographically before delivery to the IRMS via the ConFlo IV (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010a).
  • Isodat: Thermo Fisher software for analytical sequence design, instrument control, and raw isotope data export (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010b).
  • Linearity correction: Adjustment applied to account for the dependence of measured \(\delta\) values on peak amplitude at the detector (Paul et al., 2007).
  • Reference material (RM): A substance with certified or consensus \(\delta\) values used to anchor measured values to the VPDB or AIR scale; at least two RMs spanning the expected sample range are required for two-point scale normalization (Coplen, 2011; Werner & Brand, 2001).
  • Scale normalization: Two-point (or multi-point) correction that maps measured \(\delta\) values to the international isotope-ratio scale using certified reference materials (Coplen, 2011; Paul et al., 2007).
  • Super-user: A staff or faculty member qualified to run the IRMS and perform minor maintenance (gas replacement, reactor exchange, etc.).
  • Tin capsule: 5 × 9 mm pressed-tin container used to encapsulate solid samples; tin ignites in the O2 pulse, raising combustion temperature briefly to ensure complete combustion.
  • Zero-enrichment test: Reference gas on/off pulses used to verify that the IRMS measures consistent \(\delta\) values before analysis; performed during instrument warm-up.

5 Safety and Hazards

WarningHazards
  • Pressurized gases: He, O2, N2, CO2. Verify cylinder pressures before each session. He tank pressure must be above 500 psi, regulated to 50 psi.
  • Hot surfaces: The Flash EA combustion reactor operates above 1000 °C. Use caution when handling ash fingers or reactor tubes.
  • Sharps: Syringes, tweezers, and capsule-crimping tools can cause puncture or cut injuries.
  • Chemical: Chromium oxide, silvered cobaltous/cobaltic oxide, and reduced copper used in the reactor packing are irritants; follow standard laboratory handling precautions.
ImportantUser Responsibilities

The lab manager verifies that methods are working properly, but Certified Users are responsible for preparing their own samples, creating run sequences, overseeing runs, and conducting data reduction.

7 Sample Preparation

7.1 Drying and Grinding

  1. Weigh soil or sediment samples into aluminum crucibles.
  2. Oven-dry at 105 °C until constant mass.
  3. Grind dried samples to a fine, homogeneous powder.

7.2 Elemental Pre-screening

Submit a subset of samples to elemental analysis (EA) to determine %C and %N before committing the full batch. This determines the correct sample mass to target the optimal signal range.

7.3 Sample Weighing

The target sample mass depends on %C and %N concentrations (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010a):

Typical %C Target Sample Mass Notes
> 5% C 3–5 mg Dilute if signal is too high
1–5% C 7–15 mg Standard range
< 1% C 15–25 mg May require longer run times
  1. Fold a tin capsule using tweezers (clean hands; no skin oils).
  2. Weigh the target sample mass into the capsule.
  3. Add WO3 if required for combustion aids.
  4. Crimp the capsule tightly so no sample can escape during handling.
  5. Place crimped capsules in sequence order in a cell-well plate.
  6. Record the exact mass of each sample in the data reduction spreadsheet.
TipTip: Clean Tweezers and Capsules

Oils and residue from fingers contaminate tin capsules, introducing C and N blanks. Handle capsules only with clean, dry tweezers (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010a).

8 Instrument Warm-Up and Pre-Analysis Checks

See SOP-L208 for the complete warm-up procedure, gas pressure verification, zero-enrichment test, and linearity check. The Flash EA should be at operating temperature and passing all pre-analysis tests before loading samples.

9 Sequence Design

  1. Open Isodat on the acquisition computer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 2010b).
  2. Create a new sequence with the following elements in order:
    • Empty capsule blanks (4–6): establish blank baseline
    • Reference materials (2–3 each of two or more certified RMs): for scale normalization (Coplen, 2011)
    • Conditioning samples (2–3): first samples in the run; typically sacrificial or low-priority
    • Unknown samples (bracketed by RMs every 10–12 unknowns)
    • Closing reference materials (2–3 of each RM): for drift correction (Paul et al., 2007)
  3. Enter sample names, masses, and expected %C/%N for each row.
  4. Assign the correct Isodat method to the sequence.
  5. Save the sequence file before starting.

10 Running the Analysis

  1. Verify carrier gas pressure (He > 500 psi, regulated to 50 psi).
  2. Load capsules into the autosampler carousel in sequence order.
  3. Press Start Sequence in Isodat.
  4. Monitor the first 3–4 samples to confirm:
    • N2 and CO2 peaks are within the expected mass range
    • Peak shapes are symmetric and well-separated
    • Reference material \(\delta\) values are within ±0.3‰ of expected values (Werner & Brand, 2001)
  5. If values are drifting, stop the run, identify the cause, and restart after correction.

11 Shutdown

  1. After the final sample, allow the sequence to complete fully.
  2. Follow the shutdown procedure in SOP-L208.
  3. Export the raw data file from Isodat and save to the project directory.

12 Data Reduction

Data reduction follows the protocol in SOP-L414, which covers:

  1. Blank correction (empty tin capsule contributions) (Paul et al., 2007)
  2. Linearity correction (\(\delta\) vs. peak area regression)
  3. Two-point scale normalization to the VPDB / AIR scale using certified RMs (Coplen, 2011)
  4. Drift correction (linear regression through bracketing RMs)
  5. Uncertainty estimation and quality flags

16 Training Record

Before running analyses independently, personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP and SOP-L208 in full
  2. Be assigned a certification level by the Lab PI
  3. Observe a Super-User or Certified User run a complete sequence from sample loading through data reduction
  4. Run a supervised batch of at least 10 unknown samples with RM checks, achieving results within ±0.3‰ of expected values for both C and N
  5. Be signed off by the Lab PI or designated Super-User
