Flash EA Soil Combustion for IRMS Analysis

Authors
Affiliations

Marc Los Huertos

Environmental Analysis, Pomona College

Kyle McCarty

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This SOP describes the complete workflow for analyzing soil and sediment samples for \(\delta^{15}\)N, \(\delta^{13}\)C, and elemental C and N content using the Thermo Scientific Flash IRMS Elemental Analyzer (EA) coupled to the Delta V Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometer (IRMS) through the ConFlo IV continuous-flow interface. It covers sample preparation, instrument warm-up, sequence creation, running the analysis, shutdown, and data reduction.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: Soil, sediment, and plant tissue samples analyzed for carbon and nitrogen isotopic composition (\(\delta^{13}\)C, \(\delta^{15}\)N) and elemental concentrations (%C, %N) by combustion EA-IRMS.
  • Does not apply to: Sulfur isotope analysis (NCS mode requires a dedicated reactor and column; see separate SOP), GasBench carbonate analysis, TC/EA hydrogen and oxygen isotope analysis, or liquid samples.
  • Location: David W. and Claire B. Oxtoby Environmental Isotope Lab, SGM Rm 135, Pomona College. Sample preparation takes place in the wet lab, SGM Rm 133.

4. Safety and Hazards

WarningHazards
  • Pressurized gases: He, O2, N2, CO2. Verify cylinder pressures before each session. He tank pressure should be above 500 psi, regulated to 50 psi.
  • Hot surfaces: The Flash EA combustion reactor operates at high temperatures. Use caution when handling ash fingers or reactor tubes.
  • Sharps: Syringes, tweezers, and capsule-crimping tools can cause puncture or cut injuries.
  • Chemical reagents: Chromium oxide, silvered cobaltous/cobaltic oxide, reduced copper wires, and quartz wool are used in reactor packing. Consult SDS sheets before handling.

Required PPE

Emergency Procedures

In case of gas leak, fire, or injury, follow the procedures in SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety. Contact the lab manager or Dr. Marc Los Huertos immediately.

7. Sample Preparation

7.1 Drying and Grinding

  1. Weigh soil or sediment cores into aluminum crucibles.
  2. Oven-dry at 105 °C until constant mass.
  3. Grind dried samples to a fine, homogeneous powder.

7.2 Elemental Pre-screening

Submit a subset of samples to elemental analysis (EA) to determine %C and %N. Use these results to calculate the target mass for isotopic analysis. An online calculator is available at http://stableisotopefacility.ucdavis.edu/sample-weight-calculator.html.

7.3 Capsule Weighing

  1. Tare the microbalance with an empty tin capsule.
  2. Weigh the target mass of sample into the capsule (typically 0.5–20 mg, depending on %C and %N).
  3. If required by your method, add approximately 2 parts WO3.
  4. Crimp the capsule tightly into a ball, removing all air pockets. A poorly sealed capsule can introduce atmospheric N2 and ruin the measurement.
  5. Place each capsule into a labeled cell well.
  6. Prepare standards and blanks in the same manner. The number of standards depends on your data-correction scheme, but plan for at least 2–3 replicates of each standard bracketing the sample set.
Tip

Keep a detailed log of sample IDs, target masses, and actual masses. You will need this for data reduction.

8. Instrument Warm-Up and Pre-Analysis Checks

8.1 Starting a Cold Instrument

If the instrument has been powered down:

  1. Verify that all gas valves are open and compressed air is connected.
  2. Confirm the needle valve is closed.
  3. Turn on the system with the MAIN SWITCH.
  4. Power on the computer and launch Isodat.
  5. Switch on pumps at the Control Panel.
  6. In the MS State panel, switch on all heaters needed.
  7. In the Accessories toolbar of Isodat, access the ion source settings.
  8. Allow 24–48 hours for the instrument to stabilize.

8.2 Gas Checks

Verify pressures at both tank farms:

  • Tank Farm 1 (west wall, north): He (>500 psi, regulated at 50 psi), N2, CO2, O2.
  • Tank Farm 2 (west wall, south): H2, CO, H2/He mix, H2/CO2 mix, SO2.

Confirm gas flows are within specification for the CN method.

8.3 Instrument Status

  • No red or yellow warning lights on the IRMS front panel.
  • Vacuum within acceptable range.
  • High voltage (HV), box, and trap values reading back normally in Isodat.

8.4 Purge, Bake-Out, and Temperature Ramp

  1. Purge atmospheric gases from the system.
  2. Perform a column bake-out if the instrument has been idle.
  3. Initialize the temperature ramp for the combustion reactor.

8.5 Background Checks

Check residual gas backgrounds in the ion source. Elevated backgrounds can indicate leaks or contamination:

m/z Species Possible Cause
2 He\(^{2+}\) High He pressure or source tuning issue
18 H2O\(^{+}\) Moisture leak or insufficient bake-out
28 N2\(^{+}\) Air leak
40 Ar\(^{+}\) Air leak
44 CO2\(^{+}\) Residual CO2 or combustion byproduct

8.6 Zero-Enrichment (On/Off) Tests

Perform reference gas on/off tests to verify the instrument is within specification. Each on/off cycle takes approximately 10 minutes. You will almost always need multiple cycles before the instrument stabilizes.

Caution

The instrument can drift out of specification even after initially meeting it, especially if it has been idle. Do not skip this step, and do not rush it.

8.7 Tuning

For continuous-flow analysis, tune the IRMS for linearity (consistent ion ratios across a range of signal intensities) rather than maximum sensitivity. This minimizes the dependence of measured \(\delta\) values on peak amplitude.

9. Sequence Preparation in Isodat

  1. Open the Acquisition module in Isodat and create a new sequence file.
  2. Define the total number of positions (samples + standards + blanks). Each autosampler carousel holds 32 wells; up to three carousels can be stacked for 94 positions total.
  3. Select the appropriate method (e.g., the NC dual method). If no suitable method exists, contact the lab manager.
  4. Verify that Peak Center has a green check mark.
  5. Enter sample identifiers in the “Identifier 1” column.
  6. Use the autofill function to assign the method to all rows.

10. Running the Sequence

  1. Load the autosampler carousel(s) into the Flash EA.

  2. Double-check that each carousel position matches your sequence.

  3. Click Start.

  4. Save the sequence file using the lab naming convention:

    YYYYMMDD_Project_SequenceID_Username.seq

10.1 Approximate Run Times

Analysis Time per Sample (approx.)
\(\delta^{15}\)N only ~4–5 min
\(\delta^{13}\)C only ~5–6 min
NC dual method ~7–8 min
NCS triple analysis ~11–12 min

Times include an additional minute for peak centering and magnet switching.

Warning

Plan your time carefully. A full 94-sample run on the NC dual method takes roughly 12 hours. Make sure you have enough gas, enough time, and a plan for monitoring the run. Do not start a run you cannot finish.

11. End-of-Run Shutdown and Cleanup

  1. Confirm the sequence completed successfully in Isodat.
  2. Follow the instrument-specific shutdown procedure (contact the lab manager if unsure).
  3. Log your gas usage and note any issues in the lab logbook.
  4. Remove your samples and personal items from the lab.
  5. Clean up after yourself. Dispose of Kimwipes, weighing papers, and other waste. Wipe down the balance and work surfaces.
  6. Push in your chair. Keep the lab tidy and free of obstructions.

12. Data Reduction

Code 
# =============================================================================
# Data reduction workflow (placeholder)
# =============================================================================
# 1. Export raw results from Isodat as a CSV.
# 2. Read into R and merge with your sample log (masses, IDs).
# 3. Apply blank correction (if applicable).
# 4. Apply linearity correction using the on/off test slope.
# 5. Normalize delta values to the international scale using your
#    bracketing standards (two-point or multi-point calibration).
# 6. Calculate %C and %N from peak areas using standard curves.
# 7. Flag any QC failures (see Section 13).
# =============================================================================

library(tidyverse)

# --- Example skeleton ---
raw <- read_csv("data/raw/YYYYMMDD_Project_SeqID_User.csv")

# Identify standards vs. samples
raw <- raw %>%
 mutate(type = case_when(
   str_detect(Identifier1, "LOCSS|HOCSS|Urea|USGS") ~ "standard",
   str_detect(Identifier1, "blank|BLK")              ~ "blank",
   TRUE                                               ~ "sample"
 ))

# Blank correction
blank_avg <- raw %>%
 filter(type == "blank") %>%
 summarize(across(c(Area28, Area44), mean, na.rm = TRUE))

raw <- raw %>%
 mutate(
   Area28_corr = Area28 - blank_avg$Area28,
   Area44_corr = Area44 - blank_avg$Area44
 )

# Two-point normalization (example for d13C)
std_known <- tibble(
 Identifier1 = c("USGS40", "USGS41a"),
 d13C_true   = c(-26.39, 36.55)
)

std_measured <- raw %>%
 filter(Identifier1 %in% std_known$Identifier1) %>%
 group_by(Identifier1) %>%
 summarize(d13C_meas = mean(d13C_raw, na.rm = TRUE)) %>%
 left_join(std_known, by = "Identifier1")

# Linear regression: true = m * measured + b
fit <- lm(d13C_true ~ d13C_meas, data = std_measured)

raw <- raw %>%
 mutate(d13C_calibrated = predict(fit, newdata = tibble(d13C_meas = d13C_raw)))

# Write results
write_csv(raw, "data/processed/YYYYMMDD_Project_SeqID_calibrated.csv")
Note

The R code above is a starting template. Adapt it to your specific standards, correction scheme, and column names from Isodat. Consult the lab manager or your PI if you are unsure about the calibration approach.

13. QC/QA Criteria

  • Blanks: Peak areas for blanks should be negligible relative to sample peaks. If blank areas exceed 5% of the smallest sample peak, investigate contamination.
  • Standard reproducibility: Replicate standards within a run should agree within 0.2 per mil for \(\delta^{13}\)C and \(\delta^{15}\)N.
  • Linearity: The slope of \(\delta\) vs. amplitude from on/off tests should be small (instrument-specific threshold; consult the lab manager).
  • Drift: Compare early and late standards in the sequence. If drift exceeds 0.3 per mil, apply a drift correction or re-run.

14. Troubleshooting

Symptom Possible Cause / Action
High m/z 28 background Air leak; check fittings and reactor seals
High m/z 18 background Moisture; extend bake-out time
Poor reproducibility on standards Capsule sealing issue; reactor exhaustion
No peaks in chromatogram Gas flow interrupted; check He supply
Sequence stops mid-run Autosampler jam; check carousel alignment

If you cannot resolve an issue, stop the run and contact the lab manager. Do not attempt reactor replacement or major maintenance unless you are a certified super-user.

15. Time Management Reminders

Running the IRMS takes a lot of time. Set aside multiple, relatively uninterrupted days. Key time sinks to plan for:

  • Instrument bake-out and stabilization (hours to overnight).
  • Background and zero-enrichment checks (variable; budget at least 1–2 hours).
  • Sample weighing (plan for 1–2 minutes per capsule when starting out).
  • The actual analytical run (see Section 6.3 for per-sample times).
  • Data reduction (budget a full session).

Instrument time is first-come, first-serve, with priority given to PIs. Schedule time with the lab technician (kyle.mccarty@pomona.edu) in advance. Expect delays if you are scheduled immediately after another user.

16. Definitions

  • ConFlo IV: The interface hub connecting all peripherals, reference gases, and the IRMS. It also handles dilution of sample and reference gases to keep signals within the working range.
  • Delta V IRMS: The ThermoFisher Delta V Isotope Ratio Mass Spectrometer in the Oxtoby Lab.
  • Flash IRMS EA: The elemental analyzer peripheral that combusts solid samples to produce N2, CO2, and (optionally) SO2 for isotopic analysis.
  • Linearity: The relationship between measured \(\delta\) values and signal amplitude. A well-tuned instrument shows minimal dependence of \(\delta\) on peak size.
  • Super-User: A staff or faculty member qualified to run the IRMS and perform minor maintenance (gas replacement, reactor exchange, etc.).
  • User: A student, staff, or faculty member certified to prepare samples and run the IRMS without supervision.
  • Zero-Enrichment Test: Reference gas on/off pulses used to verify that the IRMS is measuring consistent \(\delta\) values before analysis.
