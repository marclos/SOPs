Eureka Manta2 Multiprobe for Water Quality Testing

Author
Affiliation

Haley Land-Miller

EA Program, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-13

1. Purpose

This SOP describes the procedures for calibrating, operating, and maintaining the Eureka Manta2 Sub2 Multiprobe for water quality measurements including temperature, pH, depth, conductivity, and dissolved oxygen (mg/L and % saturation).

2. Scope

  • Applies to: Water quality measurements in lakes, streams, rivers, and other surface water bodies
  • Does not apply to: Groundwater monitoring wells (requires different deployment procedures); laboratory bench-top water analysis
  • Location: All EA Program field water sampling sites

3. Definitions

Probe (Sonde): The entire Manta2 apparatus that includes the five sensors.

Sensor: One of the individual detectors on the probe, including temperature, pH, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, and the reference electrode.

SRF (Sensor Response Factor): A calibration diagnostic value indicating how the sensor responded to the standard. Acceptable ranges are specific to each sensor (see calibration sections below).

4. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Lab PI Approve protocol, ensure training compliance
Field Lead Verify calibration before deployment, coordinate logistics
Trained Personnel Calibrate, operate, and clean the probe per this SOP
New Students Complete training before independent use

Required Training

Researchers using this SOP should be trained on SOP-F01 Field Safety.

5. Safety and Hazards

WarningHazards
  • Biological/Chemical: Water bodies may be contaminated; avoid ingestion and direct skin contact
  • Physical: Slippery surfaces near water; risk of submersion; electrical components near water
  • Chemical: pH standards (pH 4, 7, 10) and conductivity standard are mild irritants

Required PPE

Related SOPs

Refer to SOP-F01 Field Safety for general field safety, transportation, and emergency response.

8. Interferences

  • Calibration is essential for obtaining meaningful data. Always calibrate before each field outing.
  • The reference electrode must be maintained by refilling the reference electrolyte once every few months. Failure to do so causes unreliable pH readings.
  • When sampling in streams or rivers, take downstream measurements first to avoid your movements and presence affecting the readings. Stand on the bank or downstream of the measurement point while the probe is in the water.

9. Pre-Field Preparation

9.1 Charging

Charge the Amphibian 2 portable PC before going into the field. The charger plugs into the round hole on the bottom edge of the Amphibian. Allow several hours or charge overnight.

9.2 Probe Setup for Calibration

  1. Remove the blue storage plug from the probe and cover the plug with a plastic protector
  2. Insert the underwater cable to connect the Amphibian and the Manta2
  3. Turn on the Amphibian by pressing the power button (bottom right corner)
  4. Press the Windows icon (lower left), then select Amp_2_2_6 (or use shortcut button P1)
  5. Once the interface loads, press “Manta2” on the lower left bar and select “Calibrate”

9.3 pH Calibration (Three-Point)

  1. Select “pH units” from the Manta2 calibration menu
  2. Input the value of your first standard (e.g., 7.00) in the unshaded box
  3. Unscrew the blue cap from the larger cap, leaving the clear plastic cup around the sensors in place
  4. Pour a small amount of the first standard into the cup (probe facing upward), cap it, and shake to rinse the sensors. Repeat twice, pouring used standard into a waste beaker each time
  5. Fill the cup with approximately 1 inch of the standard
  6. Wait for the red line on the Amphibian chart to stabilize (flatten), then press “Ok”
  7. When prompted for the second standard, rinse twice with DI water, then twice with the second standard, fill to 1 inch, and repeat
  8. Select the option for three-point calibration and repeat with the third standard
  9. Accept the calibration when the SRF falls within the acceptable range (consult Prof. Los Huertos if uncertain)

9.4 Conductivity Calibration (One-Point)

  1. Rinse the probe twice with DI water to remove any residual pH standard
  2. Tap “Manta2” > “Calibrate” > “spCond mS/cm”
  3. Input 2.765 in the unshaded box
  4. Rinse twice with conductivity standard, then fill the cup to 1 inch
  5. Wait for the red line to stabilize, then press “Ok”
  6. The SRF should be approximately 110–120. If outside this range, recalibrate. If still outside the range after recalibration, contact Prof. Los Huertos
Note

Conductivity standard can be disposed of down the drain. pH standards must go in a waste container.

9.5 Dissolved Oxygen Calibration

Prepare air-saturated water:

  1. Put approximately half a liter of tap water in a liter jar
  2. Shake the jar vigorously for one minute with the lid on
  3. Remove the lid and let the water stand for about five minutes to allow air bubbles to escape

Calibrate HDO %sat:

  1. Rinse the probe twice with DI water
  2. If the circulator is on (check next to “Manta2”), set it to “CircOff”
  3. Tap “Manta2” > “Calibrate” > “HDO %sat”
  4. Input the barometric pressure for your location in mm Hg (find on an online weather station; convert units if needed; round to 3 significant figures)
  5. When prompted to reset HDO to factory settings, select “No”
  6. Fill the calibration cup with aerated water until it covers the sensors by approximately 1 cm
  7. Wait a few minutes for temperature to equilibrate; the reading must be stable for 3 minutes
  8. Press “Ok” to accept. The SRF should be 100. If it differs, redo the calibration. If still not 100, contact Prof. Los Huertos.

Calibrate HDO mg/L:

  1. Immediately after %sat calibration, note the temperature on the snapshot screen
  2. Tap “Manta2” > “Calibrate” > “HDO mg/L”
  3. Use a DO saturation calculator (e.g., waterontheweb.org) to convert %sat to mg/L using the temperature and pressure values
  4. Input the mg/L value as your calibration standard
  5. Wait 20 seconds for the readout to stabilize. If stable, press “Ok”
  6. If the value changes significantly, exit, re-read %sat, and use the new value to recalibrate mg/L

10. Field Procedure

10.1 Connecting and Powering On

  1. Connect the Manta2 to the Amphibian via the underwater cable
  2. Power on the Amphibian and launch Amp_2_2_6 (shortcut: P1)
  3. Adjust screen brightness using arrow up + P3 (dimmer) and arrow up + P4 (brighter)

10.2 Enabling GPS

  1. Tap on the GPS/GNSS status gadget
  2. A satellite icon will appear on the top bar while searching
  3. Do not cover the top cap (antenna) during satellite acquisition
  4. Wait until satellites are found (check: 5 SV = 5 satellites; 3Diff/4 satellites for 3D fix; lower PDOP = better accuracy)
  5. Enable GPS at least 5 minutes before taking readings

10.3 Taking Measurements

  1. Place the weighted protective cap over the sensors
  2. Lower the probe into the water at the measurement point
  3. Allow the probe to equilibrate for at least 2 minutes before reading
  4. Take a snapshot using the top-left button on the Main screen
  5. Annotate the file using the format: Year.Month.Day.Project (e.g., 2026.05.13.StreampH)
TipTip

In flowing water, always measure downstream sites first, then work upstream to avoid disturbing the water at your measurement locations.

10.4 Disconnecting

  1. Remove the probe from the water
  2. Disconnect the underwater cable from the probe
  3. Insert the blue storage plug
  4. Cover the cable end with the plastic protector

11. Post-Field Procedures

11.1 Cleaning the Probe

  1. Clean the probe with warm soapy water (liquid dish soap or mild household cleaner)
  2. Clean sensor stems with a soft brush
  3. Rinse thoroughly with tap water
  4. Store sensors with tap water inside the cup

11.2 Downloading Data

  1. Connect the Amphibian to your PC using a USB cable
  2. A green window will appear with four options; select “File Management”
  3. In the next window, navigate to the hard drive, then select “My Documents”
  4. Locate the Excel document with your data
  5. Copy the file to your project data folder

11.3 Charging

Return the Amphibian to its charging station after each field outing.

12. Troubleshooting

Problem Solution
No power Check the green LED on the Manta2; if off, the voltage is insufficient. Try another cable.
pH SRF out of range Recalibrate with fresh standards. If still out of range, refill the reference electrolyte and contact Prof. Los Huertos.
Conductivity SRF outside 110–120 Recalibrate. If still out of range, contact Prof. Los Huertos.
HDO %sat SRF not 100 Redo the calibration with freshly aerated water.
GPS not acquiring satellites Ensure the top cap (antenna) is uncovered; move to an open area away from overhead obstructions.

13. Advanced Use

Autonomous data logging: In addition to snapshots, the Manta2 supports automatic logging at customized time intervals. See page 48 of the Manta2 Manual for setup instructions.

16. Training Record

Before using this instrument independently, personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP in full
  2. Complete SOP-F01 (Field Safety) training
  3. Observe a trained operator perform calibration and field deployment
  4. Perform a supervised calibration of all sensors
  5. Perform a supervised field deployment with data collection
  6. Be signed off by the field lead or PI
