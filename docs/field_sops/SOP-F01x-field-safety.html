1. Purpose

This SOP covers field-based research activities and class field trips, addressing transportation, weather-based, and biotic hazards. Although we cannot predict all risks and hazards, it is everyone’s responsibility to identify and mitigate potential risks associated with field work.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All field-based research activities, class field trips, and off-campus data collection
  • Does not apply to: Laboratory-only procedures (see relevant Lab SOPs)
  • Location: All field sites used by the EA Program

3. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Instructor / PI Approve field plans, ensure training compliance
Trained Personnel Follow this SOP, report deviations and hazards
New Students Complete training and document review before field work

Anyone conducting research in the field is required to have documented training before commencing field work. Learning about risks and taking the precautions described here is the first step to reducing the risks associated with field work. Anyone working in the field should identify potential risks and attempt to mitigate them.

4. Safety and Hazards

WarningGeneral Field Hazards

Reducing risks is one of our main priorities. Like many endeavors in life, field work has certain risks. However, we can reduce these risks dramatically by understanding our physical limits and how these can be tested by uncontrolled environmental factors.

We rely on each individual to be mindful of their own limitations and to advocate for themselves and others to reduce risk exposure.

Required Materials

  • First Aid Kit (kits are located in EA vehicles; see EA vehicle checklist to document their presence)
  • Cell phone(s)
  • Walkie-talkies (optional)
  • Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Estimated Time

Preparing to go in the field can take a few minutes to several hours of planning and gathering resources. A good rule of thumb is to double the time you think it will take to get ready.

5. Transportation Safety

5.1 Vehicle Requirements

  • Drivers must have valid driver’s licenses and insurance
  • Drivers using EA vehicles must be approved by the college; forms can be found in the EA Department

5.2 Planning

  • Identify meet-up and departure times and locations
  • Designate a driver
  • All state and local laws must be obeyed

5.3 Responding to Vehicle Emergencies

Refer to the college’s vehicle emergency procedures. Ensure all occupants are safe, call emergency services if needed, and report the incident to the EA Department.

6. Weather-Related Hazards

6.1 Heat Exhaustion and Stroke

WarningRisk Factors
  • Working outside of shade in desert or arid climates, especially during hot times of day
  • Lack of shade coverage or proper sun protection
  • Dehydration

Prevention:

  • Use and reapply sunscreen
  • Drink plenty of water
  • Wear hats and other protective clothing

Response:

  • Hydrate the affected person
  • Place ice packs on neck, armpits, etc.
  • If conditions worsen, leave the field and find air-conditioned conditions

6.2 Cold Conditions

Risk factors: Lack of sun, time of year, wind chill

Prevention: Proper layering and clothing; stay hydrated

Response: Eat, evacuate to warmth, get a professional evaluation

6.3 Lightning

Prevention: Check weather forecast; learn to identify cumulonimbus clouds

Response: Assume lightning position and seek shelter immediately; avoid open areas, tall isolated trees, and bodies of water

6.4 Air Quality and Pollution

Working outside when ozone levels or other pollutants are elevated has a detrimental effect on health, particularly for individuals with asthma or other respiratory conditions. Hot, dry days worsen pollution, and nearby forest fires degrade air quality.

Students with any respiratory disease should be cautious and report the condition to their instructor or laboratory supervisor.

Prevention: Check air quality index (AQI) before field work; postpone if AQI is unhealthy

Response: Leave the field if symptoms develop; seek medical attention if needed

7. Geological and Environmental Hazards

7.1 Earthquake

Tectonic movement may occur anywhere in California.

Prevention: Be aware of surroundings at all times

Response: Remain low to the ground; stay away from external building frames; avoid sinkholes, gas lines, and utility wires

7.2 Flooding

Risk factors: Proximity to bodies of water, excessive rainfall

Prevention: Check the forecast for rainfall; be aware of surroundings

Response: Find the highest point in the area; do not attempt to cross rushing water

7.3 Altitude Illness (HAPE/HACE)

Risk factors: High altitudes to which students have not had time to acclimate; reduced oxygen

Symptoms: Headache, nausea, irritability

Response: If mild symptoms, acclimate and take it easy; hydrate. If persistent vomiting and severe headache, evacuate to lower altitude immediately.

7.4 Human Debris and Waste

Illegal dumping of waste and leftover construction materials (sharp metallic objects, etc.) bring risk of injury and exposure to long-term illnesses like tetanus.

Prevention: Avoid and report; be aware of surroundings; think before interacting with unusual objects

Response: Students should be aware of their vaccination history and how to proceed if they require a tetanus shot due to injury

7.5 Toxic Waste Exposure

Exposure may occur via ingestion, inhalation, or dermal contact and can be unpredictable.

Prevention: Wear appropriate PPE; avoid contact with unknown substances

Response: Remove from exposure; decontaminate; seek medical attention

8. Biotic Hazards

The field is home to a vast diversity of organisms. Being respectful of their environment is crucial to their safety and our own.

8.1 Allergies

Important

All allergies must be recorded in medical files before the start of field work. Researchers with life-threatening allergies must bring their Epi-pen to the field and keep it accessible at all times.

Mild allergic reaction: Administer 25–50 mg of Diphenhydramine (Benadryl)

Severe allergic reaction (swelling of face/throat, respiratory distress): Administer 0.3 mg of Epinephrine; may re-administer after 5 minutes. Follow with 25–50 mg of Diphenhydramine and 20–40 mg of Prednisone. Evacuate from field site immediately.

8.2 Biotic Risk Summary

Organism Habitats Risks Prevention and Response
Mosquitos Near stagnant water Vectors for disease (e.g., Malaria) Use mosquito repellent (e.g., DEET products)
Rodents Debris, dense underbrush, burrow holes Disease, infection Do not touch rodents; if bitten, clean and disinfect
Mountain Lions Native to North/South America; active early and late in the day Severe injury Make yourself look larger; do not run; throw rocks or sticks; avoid being alone
Snakes (Rattlesnakes, Cottonmouth) Various Snakebite Walk in open areas; wear heavy boots; listen for rattle; back away slowly; if bitten, seek immediate medical attention
Spiders (Black Widow, Brown Recluse) Dark sheltered areas Spider bite, nausea Wear gloves while working; shake out clothing and bedding; go to hospital if bitten
Bees and Wasps Various Swelling, pain, allergic reaction, anaphylactic shock Avoid disturbing nests; stay calm when pursued; administer epinephrine for severe reactions; take to hospital
Fleas and Ticks Underbrush, wooded areas Lyme disease Tick check after fieldwork; use insect repellent; wear long clothing; suffocate tick with petroleum jelly before removing with tweezers
Poison Oak Riparian habitats Itchy rash; red, swollen skin Learn to identify; apply Tecnu beforehand; wash affected areas with dish soap
Stinging Nettle Riparian habitats, meadows Stinging sensation Learn to identify and avoid; use anti-itch cream if needed

9. Other Illness and Injury

9.1 Fractures and Breaks

Stabilize the injured area and avoid movement. Go to the nearest medical facility for further care.

9.2 Injury from Equipment

Handle all equipment with caution. Carefully read operating procedures. Seek help from fellow students or your instructor if you have questions or doubts. Do not use any equipment if you are unsure of how to handle it.

9.3 Chronic Conditions (Epilepsy, Diabetes, Heart Conditions)

Refer to emergency procedures. Report any chronic conditions to your supervisor. Be aware of your surroundings and be prepared to react in an emergency. Know where the first aid kit is and how to get to the nearest medical facility.

10.1 Before Departure

10.2 Supply Checklist

    • Emergency personnel
    • Lab supervisor and/or instructor
    • Lab teammates

10.3 On Site

  • Identify a time to meet up and depart (if splitting up)
  • Leave no trace: ensure that the environment remains undisturbed
  • Supervisor ensures that safety procedures are followed
  • Report any potential field hazards to supervisor

11. Emergency Response Procedure

11.1 Responding to a Medical Emergency

  1. Survey the scene
  2. Identify any potential risks that could affect other team members
  3. Do not move the injured person unless necessary
  4. Summon medical help and share the following information:
    • Suspected type of injury or illness
    • Location
    • Type of assistance required
  5. Identify a location for entry if an emergency vehicle is being summoned

11.2 Documenting the Incident

  • Identify what happened and how the injury or illness occurred
  • This information will be used to eliminate hazards and prevent future incidents
  • Report the injury or illness to the lab supervisor
