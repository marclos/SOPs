Chemical Spill Response

Author
Affiliation

Marc Los Huertos

Environmental Analysis, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This SOP provides step-by-step procedures for responding to chemical spills in EA Program laboratories. It covers how to classify a spill, when to clean it up yourself vs. when to evacuate, how to clean up minor spills by chemical class, and how to document and report the incident. This SOP is designed to be a quick reference during an emergency; read it in advance so the procedures are familiar before you need them.

For general chemical safety, storage, and waste disposal, see SOP-L003 Hazardous Materials Handling. For general lab emergency procedures (fire, earthquake, medical), see SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All chemical spills in EA Program laboratories (SGM 133, 134, 135).
  • Does not apply to: Radioactive material spills, biological spills (BSL-2 organisms), or field spills (handle per SOP-F001 and local regulations).
  • Location: All EA Program laboratory spaces.

Role Responsibility

5. Spill Classification

Before doing anything else, classify the spill. Your response depends on the classification.

5.1 Minor Spill

A spill is minor if ALL of the following are true:

  • The chemical and its hazards are known (you have read the SDS)
  • The volume is small (less than approximately 500 mL of liquid or 100 g of solid)
  • No one is injured or exposed
  • The spill is confined to a small area (benchtop or floor, not spreading)
  • You have the proper PPE and spill control materials on hand
  • The chemical is not highly toxic, volatile, flammable, or reactive at the spilled quantity

Action: Clean it up yourself using the procedures in Section 7.

5.2 Major Spill

A spill is major if ANY of the following are true:

  • Someone is injured, contaminated, or experiencing symptoms
  • The chemical is unknown
  • The volume is large (more than approximately 500 mL or 100 g)
  • The spill involves a highly toxic, volatile, flammable, or reactive material at any quantity
  • The spill is spreading and cannot be easily contained
  • You do not have proper PPE or cleanup materials
  • You are unsure whether you can handle it safely

Action: Evacuate and call for help (Section 6).

6. Major Spill Response

If in Doubt, Get Out

If you are unsure whether a spill is minor or major, treat it as major. Your safety is more important than preventing property damage.

  1. Protect yourself and others.

    • Alert everyone in the lab. Help anyone who is injured or contaminated only if you can do so without endangering yourself.
    • If chemicals are on someone’s skin, begin flushing with water immediately (safety shower for body, eyewash for eyes). Continue flushing for at least 15 minutes.
    • Remove contaminated clothing while flushing.

  2. Evacuate the area.

    • Close the lab door behind you (do not lock it).
    • Move to a safe location (hallway or outside the building).
    • Prevent others from entering the area.

  3. Call Campus Safety at x72000 or 909-621-8170.

    • State: your name, location, chemical name and approximate quantity, whether anyone is injured, and whether the spill is spreading.
    • Campus Safety will contact the Claremont Fire Department HazMat team if needed.

  4. Do not re-enter the area until Campus Safety or the HazMat team clears it.

  5. Provide information. Stay available to give responders the SDS, describe what happened, and identify anyone who was exposed.

7. Minor Spill Cleanup Procedures

7.1 General Steps (All Chemical Classes)

  1. Put on PPE before approaching the spill: safety glasses or goggles, nitrile gloves (double-glove for corrosives), lab coat. If the chemical is volatile, work in or near the fume hood and consider a respirator.
  2. Confine the spill. Use absorbent material, paper towels, or a dike of spill pillows to prevent spreading, especially toward drains.
  3. Ventilate. If the chemical is volatile, increase ventilation (open the fume hood sash, turn on room ventilation). Do not use fans that could spread the chemical.
  4. Clean up using the appropriate method for the chemical class (see below).
  5. Decontaminate. Wipe the area with soap and water or an appropriate solvent after cleanup.
  6. Dispose of waste. Place all contaminated materials (absorbent, gloves, paper towels) in a labeled hazardous waste bag. Label with the chemical name, date, and “spill cleanup residue.” Dispose per SOP-L003, Section 8.

7.2 Acids

  1. Neutralize with the Spill-X-A kit (sodium bicarbonate-based agent) or, if unavailable, with dry sodium bicarbonate.
  2. Encircle the spill, then cover and mix with the neutralizing agent using the scraper provided in the kit.
  3. The reaction will produce CO2 gas and heat. Wait for effervescence to stop.
  4. Test pH of the residue with pH paper. Continue adding neutralizer until pH is between 6 and 8.
  5. Scoop the paste-like residue into a labeled waste bag.

7.3 Bases (Caustics)

  1. Neutralize with the Spill-X-C kit (citric acid-based agent) or, if unavailable, with dilute acetic acid or citric acid solution.
  2. Encircle, cover, and mix.
  3. Test pH. Continue until pH is between 6 and 8.
  4. Scoop residue into a labeled waste bag.

7.4 Organic Solvents

  1. Eliminate ignition sources (turn off hot plates, Bunsen burners, electrical equipment near the spill).
  2. Absorb with the Spill-X-S kit or, if unavailable, with vermiculite, dry sand, or commercial sorbent pads.
  3. Do not use paper towels for large solvent spills (fire risk).
  4. Scoop the absorbed material into a labeled waste bag.

7.5 Mercury

Mercury spills require special procedures due to the toxicity of mercury vapor.

  1. Do not touch mercury with bare hands.
  2. Use the mercury spill kit (located TBD – confirm location): aspirator bulb, specialized mercury sorbent powder, and sealable container.
  3. Collect visible droplets with the aspirator bulb.
  4. Sprinkle mercury sorbent powder over the area and sweep into the sealable container.
  5. Contact Campus Safety for proper disposal.
  6. Ventilate the area thoroughly.

7.6 Dry Chemical Powders

  1. Avoid generating dust: do not sweep vigorously.
  2. Dampen the powder lightly with water (unless the chemical is water reactive – check the SDS first).
  3. Scoop into a labeled waste bag.
  4. Wipe the surface with a damp paper towel.

8. Personal Contamination

8.1 Skin Contact

  1. Remove contaminated clothing immediately.
  2. Flush the affected skin with water for at least 15 minutes (use the safety shower for large areas).
  3. Do not use chemical neutralizers on skin.
  4. Seek medical attention if irritation, burns, or symptoms develop.

8.2 Eye Contact

  1. Go to the eyewash station immediately.
  2. Hold eyelids open and flush both eyes for at least 15 minutes, even if only one eye was affected.
  3. Remove contact lenses during flushing if possible.
  4. Seek medical attention.

8.3 Inhalation

  1. Move to fresh air immediately.
  2. If symptoms develop (dizziness, coughing, difficulty breathing), call Campus Safety and seek medical attention.
  3. Inform responders of the chemical involved.

9. Incident Reporting and Documentation

Every spill, regardless of size, must be documented.

9.1 Immediate Notification

  • Minor spills: Notify the lab manager and PI by email within 24 hours.
  • Major spills: Campus Safety is notified in real time (Section 6). The lab manager and PI must also be informed immediately.
  • Injuries or exposures: Report to Campus Safety and the PI immediately. Complete a Pomona College Incident/Injury Report.

9.2 Spill Incident Report

Complete the following form (available in the lab or electronically) and submit to the lab manager within 48 hours:

Field Information to Record
Date and time When the spill occurred
Location Room, bench, fume hood number
Reporter name Who discovered the spill
Chemical(s) involved Name, concentration, approximate quantity
Cause How the spill happened
Classification Minor or major
Injuries/exposures Names, symptoms, treatment provided
Cleanup method Agent used, pH of residue, waste disposal
Corrective actions Steps taken to prevent recurrence
Witnesses Names of anyone present

9.3 Post-Incident Review

For major spills or any spill involving injury, the PI will convene a brief review within one week to identify root causes and update procedures if needed.

10.1 Kit Contents

Each EA Program lab should have the following spill materials accessible:

Item Location Check Frequency
Spill-X-A (acid neutralizer) Under fume hood Monthly
Spill-X-C (caustic neutralizer) Under fume hood Monthly
Spill-X-S (solvent absorbent) Under fume hood Monthly
Mercury spill kit TBD Monthly
pH paper (wide range) With spill kits Monthly
Absorbent pads / pillows Under bench Monthly
Scraper and dustpan With spill kits Monthly
Labeled waste bags With spill kits Monthly
Nitrile gloves (extra pairs) With spill kits Monthly

10.2 Monthly Check

The lab manager (or designated student) inspects spill kits on the first Monday of each month. Verify that all items are present and not expired. Replenish as needed. Record the check date on the spill kit inspection log posted on the kit.

11. Definitions

SDS
Safety Data Sheet. A document describing a chemical’s hazards, properties, safe handling, and emergency procedures. Located on the counter near the east entrance and in the PPE shelves near the entrance of SGM 133.
Spill-X-A / Spill-X-C / Spill-X-S
Commercial spill control kits manufactured by Ansul (now Johnson Controls). A = acid neutralizer, C = caustic neutralizer, S = solvent absorbent.

14. Training Record

Before working independently in the lab, all personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP in full
  2. Complete SOP-L002 and SOP-L003 training
  3. Locate all spill kits, the safety shower, and the eyewash station in their assigned lab
  4. Walk through a simulated minor spill cleanup with the lab manager
  5. Be signed off by the lab manager or PI
