Glassware Cleaning and Preparation

Author
Affiliation

Marc Los Huertos

EA Program, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This procedure describes the inspection, routine cleaning, acid washing, drying, and storage of laboratory glassware used in the EA Program laboratory. Properly cleaned glassware is essential for reliable analytical results; residual detergent, organic films, or trace metals can introduce systematic bias into water quality, soil extraction, and instrumental analyses. The cleaning tier required depends on the downstream analysis, ranging from routine detergent washing for general use to trace-metal-grade acid washing for ICP-MS and other sensitive techniques.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All reusable borosilicate glass labware (beakers, Erlenmeyer flasks, graduated cylinders, volumetric flasks, watch glasses, funnels, and media bottles) used in the EA Program laboratory
  • Does not apply to: Disposable glassware (e.g., serological pipettes), plastic labware, quartz cuvettes, or specialty items requiring manufacturer-specific cleaning (e.g., NMR tubes, electrochemistry cells)
  • Location: Glassware washing station, EA Program laboratory, SGM 133

4. Safety and Hazards

WarningHazards
  • Physical: Broken or chipped glassware can cause lacerations. Hot glassware from the drying oven (105 C) causes burns on contact.
  • Chemical: Concentrated acids (HCl, HNO3) used in acid baths are corrosive and emit irritating fumes. Alconox detergent is a mild irritant.

Required PPE

Emergency Procedures

  • Acid splash to skin or eyes: Flush immediately with water at the eyewash station or safety shower for at least 15 minutes. Refer to SOP-L003 Hazardous Materials Handling.
  • Broken glass injury: Apply pressure with a clean cloth. If the cut is deep or contaminated with chemicals, seek medical attention.
  • Acid spill: Neutralize small spills with sodium bicarbonate; for large spills, use the spill kit and evacuate the area. See SOP-L003.

6. Procedure

6.1 Inspection Before Use

  1. Inspect every piece of glassware before use. Check for chips, cracks, stars, or deep scratches, especially around rims, spouts, and the base
  2. Discard any piece that is broken, chipped, starred, or badly scratched. Place it in the designated broken-glass box (rigid cardboard container labeled “BROKEN GLASS”), not in the regular trash
  3. Check volumetric glassware (flasks, graduated cylinders) for legible graduation marks. Do not use glassware with faded or unreadable scales.
TipTip

Run your fingernail gently around the rim of a beaker or flask. If you feel a nick or irregularity, the piece should be discarded. Chipped rims create stress points that can propagate into cracks during heating.

6.2 Routine Cleaning (General Laboratory Use)

Use this procedure for glassware that will be used for general chemistry, soil extractions (KCl, K2SO4), gravimetric analyses, and other procedures that do not require trace-metal cleanliness.

  1. Rinse the glassware with tap water immediately after use to prevent residues from drying
  2. Prepare a warm solution of Alconox detergent (approximately 10 g per liter of warm tap water) in the wash basin
  3. Submerge the glassware and scrub all interior and exterior surfaces with an appropriately sized brush. Use brushes with wooden or plastic handles only; metal-handled brushes and worn brushes where the spine contacts the glass will scratch the surface
  4. Pay special attention to the interior bottom of beakers and flasks, where residues accumulate
  5. Rinse thoroughly under running tap water, inverting and filling each piece at least three times to remove all detergent
  6. Perform a final rinse with three changes of deionized (DI) water
  7. Invert the glassware on a peg-style drying rack or place upright in the drying oven at 105 C for 2–4 hours (see SOP-L105)
ImportantCritical

Do not allow acid to contact glassware before detergent is thoroughly removed. Acid reacting with residual soap creates a hydrophobic film that is difficult to remove and will interfere with subsequent analyses.

6.3 Acid Washing (Trace-Metal and Nutrient Analysis)

Use this procedure for glassware destined for trace-metal analysis (ICP-MS, ICP-OES, XRF digestions), nutrient analysis, or any procedure where the SOP specifies “acid-washed glassware.” All acid bath work must be performed in the fume hood.

  1. Complete the routine cleaning procedure (Section 6.2) first. Glassware must be detergent-free before entering the acid bath.
  2. Prepare the acid bath in a dedicated polyethylene tub inside the fume hood:
    • General acid bath: 10% (v/v) HCl in DI water
    • Trace-metal acid bath: 10% (v/v) HNO3 (Trace Metal grade) in DI water
  3. Submerge the clean glassware completely in the acid bath, ensuring no air bubbles are trapped inside
  4. Cover the tub with its lid and soak for a minimum of 4 hours; overnight soaking (12–24 hours) is preferred
  5. Using acid-resistant gloves and splash goggles, remove the glassware from the acid bath
  6. Rinse each piece with at least five changes of DI water. For trace-metal work, use Type I (18.2 M-ohm) water from the Milli-Q system for the final three rinses.
  7. Place the rinsed glassware on a clean drying rack or in the drying oven. Do not use paper towels or Kimwipes to dry the interior of acid-washed glassware, as fibers introduce contamination.
  8. Once dry, cover the opening of each piece with a clean watch glass, aluminum foil, or parafilm and label it “ACID WASHED” with the date
Note

The acid bath should be replaced when it becomes visibly discolored or after approximately 20 uses, whichever comes first. Record replacement dates in the acid bath log on the fume hood.

6.4 New Glassware

New borosilicate glassware is slightly alkaline from the manufacturing process. Before first use in quantitative analyses:

  1. Soak the new glassware in a 1% HCl or 1% HNO3 solution for several hours
  2. Proceed with the routine cleaning procedure (Section 6.2)
  3. For trace-metal applications, also complete the acid-washing procedure (Section 6.3)

6.5 Stubborn Residues and Special Cases

Residue Type Cleaning Method
Dried organic matter Soak overnight in Alconox; scrub with brush
Mineral scale or precipitates Soak in 10% HCl for 4+ hours
Grease or stopcock lubricant Rinse with acetone in the fume hood, then routine wash
Biological residues (agar, culture media) Autoclave before washing; see SOP-L002
Unknown or heavily stained Soak in 10% HNO3 overnight; if persistent, contact the lab manager
WarningDo Not Use Chromic Acid

Chromic acid (potassium dichromate in sulfuric acid) was historically used as a glassware cleaner but is a potent human carcinogen and is prohibited in this laboratory. Use the acid-wash procedures described above instead.

6.6 Drying and Storage

  1. Air-dry inverted on a peg rack, or oven-dry at 105 C for 2–4 hours (see SOP-L105)
  2. Do not oven-dry volumetric glassware (volumetric flasks, graduated cylinders). Thermal expansion can permanently alter the calibration. Air-dry volumetric glassware only.
  3. Store clean, dry glassware in designated cabinets with shelf liner
  4. Store glassware inverted or covered to prevent dust accumulation
  5. Keep acid-washed glassware in a separate, labeled section of the cabinet or on a dedicated shelf to prevent accidental use for non-critical applications

7. Quality Control and Acceptance Criteria

Water-Break Test

The water-break test is a simple visual check for cleanliness:

  1. Rinse the interior of the glassware with DI water
  2. Observe the drainage pattern as water sheets off the surface
  3. Pass: Water forms a uniform, unbroken film across the entire interior surface
  4. Fail: Water beads up, forms droplets, or breaks into rivulets, indicating residual organic contamination or grease

If the water-break test fails, repeat the cleaning procedure. If it fails again after acid washing, discard the piece, as surface damage may be preventing adequate cleaning.

Routine Verification

  • Perform the water-break test on a representative sample (1 in 10 pieces) from each cleaning batch
  • For acid-washed glassware, run a DI water blank through the glassware and analyze it on the target instrument (e.g., ICP-MS) to confirm background levels are below method detection limits
  • Record cleaning batch dates and water-break test results in the glassware cleaning log

8. Waste Disposal

Waste Type Disposal Method
Spent acid bath (HCl or HNO3) Neutralize with sodium bicarbonate to pH 6–8, then dispose down the drain with copious water, or collect for EH&S pickup if volume is large
Broken glass Place in the rigid “BROKEN GLASS” disposal box; do not place in regular trash or recycling
Detergent wash water Drain; Alconox is biodegradable
Acetone rinse waste Collect in organic waste carboy in fume hood; EH&S pickup

10. Training Record

Before cleaning glassware independently, personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP in full
  2. Complete SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety and SOP-L003 Hazardous Materials Handling training
  3. Demonstrate proper use of brushes, detergent preparation, and the rinse sequence under supervision
  4. For acid-washed glassware: demonstrate proper fume hood use, acid-resistant PPE donning, and acid bath handling under supervision
  5. Perform a water-break test and have results reviewed by the lab manager
  6. Be signed off by the PI or lab manager

Appendix B: Acid Bath Preparation

10% HCl Acid Bath

  1. Place the clean polyethylene tub in the fume hood
  2. Fill the tub approximately 90% full with DI water
  3. Slowly add concentrated HCl (approximately 37%) to reach a final concentration of 10% (v/v). For a 20 L bath, add approximately 2 L of concentrated HCl to approximately 18 L of DI water.
  4. Always add acid to water, never water to acid
  5. Label the tub with: acid type, concentration, preparation date, and a hazard warning
  6. Cover with the fitted lid when not in use

10% HNO3 Acid Bath (Trace Metal Grade)

  1. Follow the same steps as above, substituting Trace Metal grade HNO3 (Fisher A509P500) for HCl
  2. Use Type I DI water from the Milli-Q system
  3. This bath is reserved for trace-metal glassware only; do not cross-contaminate with the general HCl bath
