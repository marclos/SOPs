Waste Disposal and Hazardous Waste Pickup

Author
Affiliation

Marc Los Huertos

EA Program, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This procedure describes the classification, segregation, labeling, storage, and disposal of all laboratory waste generated in EA Program laboratories, with emphasis on hazardous chemical waste requiring EH&S pickup. It consolidates and expands the waste disposal sections of SOP-L003 Hazardous Materials Handling (Sections 8.1–8.6) into a standalone reference that can be posted in the waste accumulation area and used during training.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All laboratory waste generated in EA Program laboratories (SGM 133, 134, 135), including chemical waste, biological waste, sharps, and non-hazardous solid waste
  • Does not apply to: Radioactive waste (not generated in EA Program labs); field waste (see SOP-F001); e-waste and surplus equipment (coordinate with campus surplus property office)
  • Location: All EA Program laboratory spaces

3. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Lab PI Ensure compliance with campus and regulatory waste policies; sign manifests as generator
Lab Manager Coordinate EH&S pickups, maintain satellite accumulation areas, train personnel
All Lab Personnel Segregate and label waste correctly per this SOP
Campus EH&S Collect hazardous waste, prepare manifests, arrange off-site disposal

4. Regulatory Context

Pomona College is classified as a Small Quantity Generator (SQG) of hazardous waste under RCRA (40 CFR Part 262) and California DTSC regulations. Key requirements:

  • Hazardous waste may be accumulated in satellite accumulation areas (SAAs) near the point of generation in containers of 55 gallons or less (1 quart for acutely hazardous waste)
  • Once full, containers must be moved to the central accumulation area and picked up by EH&S within 3 days
  • All containers must be labeled with the words “Hazardous Waste,” the contents, the hazard(s), and the accumulation start date
  • The generator (Lab PI) must sign the packing manifest

5. Waste Classification

5.1 Non-Hazardous Solid Waste

Waste Type Disposal
Paper, packaging, cardboard Recycling bins
Uncontaminated gloves and wipes Standard trash
Clean glassware (broken) Sharps box or wrapped cardboard, then trash
Uncontaminated soil (dried) Standard trash or return to field site
Used aluminum tins (clean) Recycle or reuse

5.2 Hazardous Chemical Waste

Any waste that is ignitable, corrosive, reactive, or toxic (per RCRA D-list), or that is a listed waste (F, K, P, or U list), must be collected and disposed as hazardous waste. Common EA Program hazardous waste streams:

Waste Stream Typical Source SOPs Container
Acid waste (HNO3, HCl, H2SO4) L410, L411, L403 Poly carboy, labeled “Acid Waste”
Base waste (NaOH, KOH) Various Separate poly carboy
Halogenated solvent waste (chloroform, DCM) L409 Amber glass, labeled “Halogenated Solvent Waste”
Non-halogenated solvent waste (acetone, ethanol, hexane) Various Separate glass or poly container
Heavy metal solutions (Pb, Cd, Cr, Hg) L410, L411 Poly carboy, labeled by metal
Formaldehyde solutions L305 (preservatives) Glass, labeled “Formaldehyde Waste”
Oxidizer solutions (H2O2, NaClO3) L412 Collect separately; do not mix with organic waste
Used oil (pump oil) Vacuum pumps Labeled container
WarningCritical Segregation Rules
  • Never mix acids with bases, oxidizers with organics, or halogenated with non-halogenated solvents.
  • Never pour hazardous waste down the drain unless explicitly approved by EH&S for that specific waste stream.
  • When in doubt, treat as hazardous and collect for EH&S pickup.

5.3 Biological Waste

Waste Type Disposal
Non-pathogenic cultures (soil microbes) Autoclave, then standard trash
Colilert/Quanti-Tray materials (L305) Standard trash after UV deactivation or autoclave
eDNA extraction waste (bead tubes, filters) Standard trash (non-infectious after extraction)
Foreign soil waste (L015) Autoclave per SOP-L015 before disposal

5.4 Sharps

Waste Type Disposal
Needles, syringes with needles Sharps container (red)
Broken glass (contaminated) Sharps container
Razor blades, scalpels Sharps container
Broken glass (uncontaminated) Cardboard box labeled “Broken Glass,” then trash
Intact glass vials (uncontaminated) Rinse and recycle, or trash

5.5 Drain-Disposable Waste

A limited set of aqueous, non-hazardous solutions may be poured down the drain with copious water, only if approved by EH&S:

  • Dilute (less than 1%) salt solutions (NaCl, KCl, K2SO4, Na2SO4) at neutral pH
  • Dilute sugar or buffer solutions at neutral pH
  • Neutralized acid or base solutions (pH 5–9) that contain no metals or other hazardous constituents
Important

If you are unsure whether a waste can go down the drain, collect it as chemical waste. The default is always to collect, never to drain.

6. Procedure

6.1 Labeling Waste Containers

  1. Every waste container in the SAA must have a hazardous waste label (available from EH&S or the lab manager) before any waste is added
  2. Fill in: waste description (chemical names and approximate percentages), hazard(s) (corrosive, flammable, toxic, etc.), accumulation start date, and laboratory location
  3. Update the label if new constituents are added to the container

6.2 Accumulating Waste

  1. Add waste to the appropriate labeled container in the SAA
  2. Keep containers closed when not actively adding waste
  3. Do not fill containers beyond 80% capacity (leave headspace for thermal expansion and safe handling)
  4. Inspect containers weekly for leaks, corrosion, or label degradation

6.3 Requesting EH&S Pickup

  1. When a container is full (or the study is complete), notify the lab manager
  2. The lab manager contacts campus EH&S to schedule a pickup
  3. Prepare a packing manifest listing: container number, contents (chemical names and approximate percentages), volume, hazard class, and generator (PI) name
  4. The PI reviews and signs the manifest
  5. Move the container to the central pickup area designated by EH&S
  6. EH&S collects the waste, provides a manifest copy, and arranges off-site treatment or disposal

6.4 Empty Container Management

  1. Triple-rinse empty reagent bottles with the appropriate solvent (water for aqueous chemicals, the same solvent for organic chemicals)
  2. Remove or deface the original label
  3. Collect rinse liquid as waste (add to the corresponding waste stream)
  4. Clean, delabeled glass bottles may be reused for non-critical purposes or recycled
  5. Do not reuse containers that held acutely hazardous (P-listed) chemicals; dispose as hazardous waste

7. Satellite Accumulation Area (SAA) Management

  • Each SAA is located at or near the point of waste generation (e.g., the fume hood where digestions are performed, the bench where extractions occur)
  • Maximum volume in an SAA: 55 gallons of hazardous waste (or 1 quart of acutely hazardous waste)
  • Containers in the SAA must be in good condition, compatible with the waste, and kept closed except when adding waste
  • Spill containment (secondary tray) is required under all liquid waste containers
  • The lab manager inspects SAAs monthly and logs the inspection on the SAA checklist posted in each area

8. Emergency Spill Response

For waste container spills, follow SOP-L004 Chemical Spill Response. Key points:

  • Contain the spill using absorbent materials appropriate to the waste type (do not use paper towels for solvent spills; use chemical absorbent pads)
  • Contaminated absorbent becomes hazardous waste; bag, label, and include in the next EH&S pickup
  • Report all spills to the lab manager and PI; document in the incident log

11. Training Record

Before handling laboratory waste independently, personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP and SOP-L003 in full
  2. Locate the SAAs in each laboratory room and identify the waste streams currently in use
  3. Demonstrate proper waste labeling (fill out a hazardous waste label correctly)
  4. Demonstrate correct segregation by assigning 5 sample waste items to the correct container
  5. Review the spill response procedure in SOP-L004
  6. Be signed off by the lab manager or PI
