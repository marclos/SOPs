Foreign Soil Handling and Import Permits

Author
Affiliation

Kathryn Hargan

EA Program, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This SOP outlines the mandatory practices for securely and safely handling, analyzing, and disposing of soils and sediments collected from outside the United States. The importation and movement of untreated foreign soil is classified by the USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) as an extremely high-risk activity. Several levels of review and approval are required before a permit can be issued. This SOP has been written to fulfill the conditions of the EA Program’s APHIS foreign soil permit.

These practices are mandatory for all personnel working with foreign soils in the EA Teaching and Research Laboratory at Pomona College. Additionally, all persons working in the laboratory, even those not directly handling foreign soils, must read this SOP and be aware of standard operations involving quarantine materials so that a safe and secure laboratory environment is maintained.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All soils and sediments collected outside the United States and its territories that are imported to or processed in the EA Program laboratory
  • Does not apply to: Domestic soils (including soils from Hawaii, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, which are not foreign countries under APHIS regulations); fully sterilized or commercially prepared soil reference materials
  • Analyses covered: ICP-MS, EA-IRMS (total C, N, S and stable isotope ratios), texture, organic matter content, and other parameters where soils can be disposed of properly after analysis
  • Location: Foreign soil storage in SGM 133 (designated cabinet); processing in SGM 134 (dedicated area)

3. Definitions

  • Foreign soils: Soils and sediments collected outside the United States and its territories
  • APHIS: The Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service, a USDA agency responsible for protecting U.S. agricultural health, regulating genetically engineered organisms, and administering quarantine regulations
  • Plant Inspection Station (PIS): A facility where imported shipments are inspected and cleared by Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and APHIS inspectors
  • CBP: U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the agency that clears imported soils and plant material for entry
  • Decontamination: A procedure that eliminates or reduces microbial contamination to a safe level with respect to transmission of infection
  • PPQ Form 508: Green and yellow APHIS shipping label for shipments of 3 lbs or less that will be treated at the PIS
  • PPQ Form 550: Black and white APHIS shipping label for shipments routed directly to a USDA-approved facility

5. Safety and Hazards

APHIS Quarantine Requirement

Soil is strictly controlled under APHIS quarantine regulations 7 CFR 330 because it can readily provide a pathway for introducing dangerous organisms into the United States. Importation of soil from foreign sources is prohibited unless authorized by APHIS under specific conditions, safeguards, and controlled circumstances described in a valid permit and/or compliance agreement. Noncompliance can result in permit revocation, fines, and legal action.

Required PPE

Storage Restrictions

  • Foreign soils must be stored only in the designated quarantine cabinet in SGM 133
  • Foreign soils must be processed only in the dedicated area in SGM 134
  • Soils must never be opened or processed outside these designated areas

Chemical Hazards

If chemicals are used during analysis (e.g., acids for digestion), review the Safety Data Sheets before beginning. For isopropyl alcohol used in decontamination, work in a well-ventilated area and keep away from ignition sources.

Emergency Procedures

Refer to SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety and SOP-L003 Hazardous Materials Handling. For spills of foreign soil, see Section 11 (Spill Cleanup).

6. Personnel and Training Requirements

All personnel working with foreign soils must complete the following before handling any quarantine material:

  1. Complete SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety training, including the annual Pomona College lab safety course
  2. Read the USDA/APHIS Compliance Agreement (current agreement number on file with the PI)
  3. Read the current Foreign Soil Permit (permit number on file with the PI)
  4. Read this SOP in full
  5. Complete SOP-L105 Drying Ovens training (required for sterilization procedures)
  6. Be signed off by the PI or lab manager
ImportantAll Lab Users

Even personnel who do not work directly with foreign soils must read this SOP so they understand the quarantine protocols in effect in SGM 133 and SGM 134. This is a condition of the APHIS permit.

NoteAPHIS Contact

For questions about the permit, shipping, or compliance, contact the local APHIS Plant Protection and Quarantine office. Verify current contact information with the PI before each import event, as personnel assignments may change.

8. Sample Collection and Import

8.1 Equipment Decontamination at the Collection Site

All field equipment must be thoroughly cleaned and disinfected of all soil residues at the collection site before packing for transport. Acceptable methods:

  • Soak in isopropyl alcohol for one hour, or
  • Soak and wash in hot water with detergent for 30 minutes

8.2 Shipping Foreign Soils

  1. Pack soil and sediment samples in sturdy, leak-proof containers. Declare contents as “Soil samples.”
  2. The primary container (test tube, vial, or bag) must be securely closed and watertight
  3. Enclose the primary container in a second, durable, watertight secondary container. Each container must be capable of containing the soil independently.
  4. Attach the appropriate APHIS shipping label to the exterior of each shipping package:
    • PPQ Form 508 (green and yellow): For shipments of 3 lbs or less that will be treated at the PIS
    • PPQ Form 550 (black and white): For shipments routed directly to the USDA-approved facility (Pomona College)
WarningLabel Placement

Packages without APHIS shipping labels on the exterior may be refused entry even if labels are enclosed inside the package.

  1. Each label is individually numbered with a distinctive barcode. The permit holder must request the appropriate labels at the time of permit issuance.
  2. Additional labels can be obtained through the “My Shipments/Labels” feature in ePermits or by emailing BlackWhiteGreenYellowlabelrequest@aphis.usda.gov

8.3 Waybills

  • PPQ Form 508 shipments require two waybills: one international waybill to direct the shipment to the PIS, and one domestic waybill (placed inside the package) to direct the shipment from the PIS to the permit holder
  • PPQ Form 550 shipments require one waybill to direct the shipment to the permit holder

The waybill must include: carrier account number or postage, permit holder name, permit number, label number, delivery address, and telephone number. If the waybill address does not match the permit address, the package may be refused entry by CBP.

8.4 Hand-Carrying Soils into the United States

Permitted articles may be hand-carried into the U.S. only if:

  1. Hand carrying was requested in the permit application and authorized in the permit conditions
  2. The permit conditions list the specific steps that must be followed
  3. If someone other than the permit holder will hand-carry, that person must be identified on the permit
  4. The authorized hand-carrier must follow all permit conditions pertaining to hand-carried shipments

8.5 Problems at the PIS or CBP

If a shipment encounters problems at the PIS or CBP, contact the local APHIS office immediately (verify current contact information with the PI). Do not attempt to resolve the issue without APHIS guidance.

NotePPQ 550 Labels Not Needed for U.S. Territories

PPQ 550 shipping labels are not required for soil originating in Hawaii, Puerto Rico, or the U.S. Virgin Islands, as these are not foreign countries under APHIS regulations.

9. Receiving, Storage, and Logging

9.1 Receiving

  1. Transport soils directly to the Biogeochemistry Lab quarantine cabinet in SGM 133. Under no circumstances may soils be opened or processed until they reach the quarantine cabinet.
  2. Open containers only inside the designated closed room area in SGM 133 or SGM 134. Take all precautions to prevent the escape of any potential pest that may be in the soil.

9.2 Logging

Upon arrival at Pomona College, log every sample in the Foreign Soils Log Book. Each entry must include:

Field Description
Sample number Year and consecutive number (e.g., 18-01)
Date of arrival YYYY-MM-DD
Origin (provenance) Country, region, site name
Collector name/initials Who collected the sample in the field
Mass (grams) As received
Reason for collection Research project or study name
Storage location SGM 133 cabinet shelf, refrigerator, or freezer
Sterilization status Unsterilized (red tag) on arrival

9.3 Labeling and Storage

  1. Samples remain sealed in original Whirl-Pak bags within Ziplock bags until processed
  2. Store bags on shelf trays within the closed quarantine cabinet. Samples must not be left on tables or elsewhere when not in use.
  3. All stored, unsterilized soils must be labeled with red tags stating “Quarantine Soil – Unsterilized”
  4. Sterilized samples must be labeled with green tags stating “Quarantine Soil – Sterilized”
  5. Any individual sample being processed outside its storage cabinet must also be clearly labeled to indicate sterilization status

9.4 Reshipment

Reshipment of foreign soil samples to other laboratories is not permitted unless the soils have been sterilized. A detailed request to reship must be submitted to the enforcing agricultural inspectors before any transfer.

10. Treatment, Sterilization, and Disposal

10.1 Sterilization Method

Soil samples must be sterilized by dry heat treatment at 250 C for 24 hours, or by one of the other approved methods documented in the Foreign Soils Log Book.

ImportantAll Residues Must Be Sterilized

All residues from processing (including ash, rinse water filter residues, and equipment rinse material) must be sterilized using the same methods as the soil itself.

10.2 Container Disposal

Once emptied, all containers (shipping boxes, plastic bags) must be either:

  • Sterilized by an approved heat treatment method, if they will be reused, or
  • Disposed of by burning

Containers may also be decontaminated by wiping or soaking with isopropyl alcohol if they are non-combustible and will be reused.

10.3 Contaminated Water Disposal

Whenever water is used in processing (including initial rinse water from contaminated equipment), the contaminated water must be disposed of by one of the following methods:

  1. Boiling the water for 1 minute
  2. Treating with a strong detergent for 30 minutes
  3. Filtering through a 100-mesh screen or suitable paper filter. The filter residues must be burned or sterilized.

10.4 Final Disposal of Soils

After all analyses are complete, sterilize remaining soils and ash at 250 C for 24 hours. Discard the sterilized material to a solid waste facility. Record the disposal date, mass disposed, and method of treatment in the Foreign Soils Log Book.

11. Spill Cleanup

If soils are spilled during handling or transport:

  1. Immediately contain the spill. Do not allow soil to spread.
  2. Recover all spilled soil material.
  3. Heat the recovered soil for sterilization at 250 C for 24 hours.
  4. Decontaminate all affected surfaces by wiping with isopropyl alcohol.
  5. Record the spill event, location, amount, and cleanup actions in the Foreign Soils Log Book.

12. Surveillance and Logging

In addition to the data logged upon arrival (Section 9.2), the Foreign Soils Log Book must be updated for each sample as it moves through processing:

Event Record
Processing Date, analysis type, analyst name
Sterilization Date, method, temperature, duration
Disposal Date, mass disposed, disposal method/location
ImportantMass Accountability

The mass of treated and disposed soils must be within 10% of the imported soil mass. Discrepancies exceeding 10% must be investigated and documented.

13. Quality Control

  • Verify that the APHIS Foreign Soil Permit is current and has not expired before any import activity
  • Confirm that APHIS shipping labels match the current permit number
  • Audit the Foreign Soils Log Book at least once per semester to verify all entries are complete and mass accountability is within 10%
  • Report any anomalies (missing samples, mass discrepancies, permit questions) to the PI immediately

15. Training Record

Before handling foreign soils independently, personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP in full
  2. Complete SOP-L002 Laboratory Safety training
  3. Complete SOP-L105 Drying Ovens training
  4. Read the current APHIS Compliance Agreement and Foreign Soil Permit (copies on file with the PI)
  5. Complete the annual Pomona College lab safety course
  6. Demonstrate proper logging, labeling (red/green tags), and sterilization procedures under supervision
  7. Be signed off by the PI

Appendix A: Sample Numbering Convention

Each sample receives a number consisting of the two-digit year and a consecutive number for that year. For example, 18-34 indicates the 34th sample logged in 2018. The numbering resets to 01 at the start of each calendar year.
