Laboratory Notebook Recording

Author
Affiliation

Marc Los Huertos

Environmental Analysis, Pomona College

Published

2026-05-27

1. Purpose

This SOP establishes the physical documentation standards for laboratory notebooks in the EA Program. A properly maintained notebook is a legal record that establishes what was done, when, by whom, and what was observed. These standards follow Good Documentation Practice (GDP) principles used in regulated laboratories and ensure that EA Program data are traceable, contemporaneous, and defensible.

This SOP covers how to record. For what to record during an analytical session, see SOP-L802 Quality Control, Section 7.1. For field notebook protocols, see SOP-F003 Field Notebook Protocols.

2. Scope

  • Applies to: All laboratory work performed under EA Program SOPs, including sample preparation, instrument operation, calibration, data reduction, and reagent/standard preparation.
  • Does not apply to: Field notebooks (see SOP-F003) or purely electronic records in R scripts and Git (see SOP-L801), though electronic records should meet the same traceability standards.
  • Location: All EA Program laboratories (SGM 133, 134, 135).

3. Responsibilities

Role Responsibility
Lab PI Set documentation standards; review notebooks
Lab Manager Issue notebooks; maintain archive; verify compliance
All Lab Personnel Maintain a notebook per this SOP
New Students Read this SOP before beginning any lab work

4. Notebook Selection

4.1 Required Type

Use a bound, duplicate carbon-copy laboratory notebook with sequentially numbered pages. Each page produces an original (white) and a carbon-copy duplicate (yellow). Acceptable examples:

  • National Brand 43-648 (200 numbered pages, carbon duplicate)
  • Boorum & Pease 09-900-2 series
  • Any equivalent bound duplicate notebook with numbered pages
ImportantWhy Carbon-Copy Duplicates?

The duplicate page creates an instant backup that can be separated from the notebook without destroying the original record. Duplicates are filed separately, so that if a notebook is lost, damaged, or in use by another researcher, the data are preserved. This is standard practice in GLP, GMP, and patent-documentation environments.

4.2 Unacceptable Notebooks

Do not use:

  • Loose-leaf binders or spiral notebooks with perforated tear-out pages
  • Composition books without numbered pages
  • Unbound paper, sticky notes, or scrap paper
  • Notebooks without duplicate (carbon-copy) pages

4.3 Issuing and Registering

  1. The lab manager issues each new notebook and records the notebook number, researcher name, project, and date issued in the Notebook Registry (a spreadsheet maintained on the lab shared drive).
  2. On the inside front cover, record: your name, contact information, project name, and the date the notebook was put into service.
  3. Reserve the first two pages for a Table of Contents. Update the TOC as you fill the notebook.

5. Writing Instruments

5.1 Required

Write all entries in permanent, non-erasable ink. Use a ballpoint pen that produces a clear impression on both the original and the carbon-copy page. Black or dark blue ink is preferred.

5.2 Not Permitted

  • Pencil (can be erased)
  • Gel ink or felt-tip pens that do not transfer reliably to the carbon copy
  • Erasable ink pens
Tip

Test your pen on a spare carbon-copy page before starting a new notebook. Press firmly enough to produce a legible duplicate, but not so hard that you tear the paper.

6. Entry Formatting

6.1 Every Entry Begins With

At the top of each new entry (or new day), record:

  1. Date in ISO format: YYYY-MM-DD (e.g., 2026-05-18)
  2. Your name (or initials, if your full name is on the inside cover and you are the sole user)
  3. Purpose of the work session in one sentence (e.g., “TOC-L calibration and K2SO4 extract analysis, Project: BFS Soils”)
  4. SOP(s) in use with revision numbers

6.2 Contemporaneous Recording

Write entries at the time the work is performed, not from memory afterward. If you record a result on a printout, sticky note, or scrap paper during an urgent moment, tape or staple the original into the notebook and initial and date it at the earliest opportunity. Never discard the original note.

6.3 Level of Detail

Write enough detail so that another trained researcher could reproduce your work using only the notebook and the referenced SOP. At minimum, include all items listed in SOP-L802, Section 7.1.

6.4 Printouts, Graphs, and Attachments

Tape or staple instrument printouts, chromatograms, calibration reports, or other documents directly into the notebook. Write across the border of the attachment and the notebook page so that it is clear the attachment was placed at that time. Initial and date each attachment.

6.5 Tables and Calculations

  • Draw tables with a ruler if practical.
  • Show all calculations, including units and intermediate steps, so that results can be verified.
  • Circle or box final results for easy identification.

6.6 Blank Space and Unused Pages

  • Do not leave large blank areas within an entry. If you finish an entry partway down a page, draw a diagonal line through the remaining blank space and initial and date it. This prevents later additions.
  • Never skip pages. If you accidentally skip a page, draw a diagonal line through it, write “INTENTIONALLY LEFT BLANK,” and initial and date it.

7. Signatures

7.1 Daily Signature

At the end of each work session (or at the end of each day if work spans a full day), sign and date the last line of your entry:

Signed: ________________ Date: __________

7.2 Witness Signature

For work that may be used in regulatory submissions, patent applications, or formal reports, have a second person (the lab manager, PI, or another trained researcher) review the entry, sign, and date it as a witness:

Witnessed and understood by: ________________ Date: __________

The witness confirms that the entry is legible, clear, and appears complete. The witness does not need to have been present during the work.

Note

For routine student projects, a daily signature by the researcher is sufficient. Witness signatures are required for thesis work, externally funded projects, and any data that may be submitted to regulatory agencies.

8. Error Corrections

Errors in a laboratory notebook are normal. The way you correct them matters.

8.1 How to Correct

  1. Draw a single horizontal line through the incorrect text so that the original remains legible.
  2. Write the correct information nearby (above, below, or in the margin).
  3. Initial and date the correction.
  4. Optionally, add a brief reason (e.g., “wrong sample ID” or “calculation error”).

8.2 What Not to Do

  • Never erase, white-out, scribble over, or obliterate any entry.
  • Never remove or tear out a page.
  • Never use correction fluid (Wite-Out, Liquid Paper, etc.).
  • Never write over existing text to change it.
Warning

Obliterated entries raise questions about data integrity. Even if the error was innocent, an unreadable original looks like a concealment. A single-line strikethrough with initials and a date is always the correct approach.

9. Handling Duplicate (Carbon-Copy) Pages

  1. After completing an entry and signing it, carefully tear out the yellow carbon-copy duplicate along the perforated edge.
  2. Place the duplicate in the Notebook Duplicates folder for your project, organized by notebook number and date.
  3. Duplicate pages are filed in the lab (SGM 133, designated cabinet).
  4. Do not take duplicate pages home or discard them.

If you do not tear out duplicates daily, do so at least weekly. Do not allow a large backlog of unremoved duplicate pages to accumulate, as the carbon transfer quality can degrade over time.

10. Electronic Supplements

Many analyses produce electronic data files (Isodat exports, TOC-L CSVs, R scripts, photographs). The notebook must reference these files:

  • Record the file name, storage location, and date for every electronic file associated with an entry.
  • Example: “Raw data exported to lab_data/BFS_Soils/2026/TOC-L/raw/20260518_BFS_run01.csv”
  • Electronic files are managed per SOP-L802, Section 7.2 and SOP-L801.

The notebook is the master index; electronic files are supplements. If there is ever a conflict between the notebook and an electronic record, investigate and resolve the discrepancy (do not simply overwrite one with the other).

11. Notebook Completion and Archiving

  1. When a notebook is full, record the date range on the front cover and spine (e.g., “2026-01 to 2026-08”).
  2. Update the Table of Contents on the first two pages.
  3. File the completed notebook in the Notebook Archive (SGM 133, labeled shelf), organized by project and date.
  4. Update the Notebook Registry spreadsheet with the completion date.
  5. Notebooks are retained for a minimum of 7 years after the end of the associated project, or longer if required by a funding agency, IRB, or regulatory body.
  6. Notebooks are the property of the EA Program and Pomona College. They must not be taken home, discarded, or removed from the archive without PI approval.

12. Common Mistakes

Mistake Consequence
Using pencil Entries can be erased; not a permanent record
Recording data on scratch paper first Scratch paper gets lost; transcription adds errors
Leaving entries unsigned Cannot confirm who performed the work
Leaving blank space without striking through Appearance that data could be inserted later
Using white-out or scribbling over errors Raises data integrity concerns
Not removing carbon-copy duplicates Backup is only useful if stored separately
Forgetting to reference electronic files Data become disconnected from context
Skipping the Table of Contents Information is hard to locate later

15. Training Record

Before beginning independent laboratory work, all personnel must:

  1. Read this SOP in full
  2. Set up a new notebook correctly (inside cover, Table of Contents, registry entry)
  3. Demonstrate a properly formatted entry including: header, data recording, attached printout, error correction, blank-space management, and signature
  4. Be signed off by the lab manager or PI
